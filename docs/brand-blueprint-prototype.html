Step 3 of 6

Pick how you say it

Jun Yuh uses three formats. Any topic can go into any of them.

1. Talking

Talking head

What it is
You speak to the camera, with captions and a few cutaway visuals.
His example
Skill: storytelling + talking head = "10/10 habits of a disgustingly good storyteller".
Best for
Skills. Teaching builds trust fastest when people hear you.
2. Carousels

Carousel

What it is
A few photos or clips with short text on each. Swipe to see the next one.
His example
Skill: discipline + carousel. "i'm so lazy" is struck out, then "i just need to do five minutes".
Best for
Skills and passions. Easy to make with photos you already have.
3. Non-talking

B-roll with text

What it is
Footage of your life with words on screen. You never speak.
His example
Story: struggle growing up + non-talking. A single line over one image: 14: "dad, it's not your fault".
Best for
Stories and moods. Good for days when you don't want to be on camera.

Good format vs. bad format

Good format
  • Starts with a strong hook that makes the audience curious
  • Shares value through education or relatability
  • Re-hooks the audience throughout to keep them watching
Bad format
  • Takes too long to get to the point
  • Has little to no value for the viewer
  • Has no structure to keep people engaged
Step 4 of 6

Three checks for every post

Run each planned post against these before you film it. The plan in the next step has a checkbox for each.

1

Strong hook

Keeps people watching past the first 3 seconds, so they reach your actual message.

2

Real value

Earns comments and shares, which push your post to more people.

3

Re-hooks

Drives high watch time, which tells the algorithm it is a quality post.

Quick test for a hook: would a stranger stop scrolling if this were the only line they saw? If not, rewrite it before you film.
