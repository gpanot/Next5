## 2. Top results and what they miss

Top of the US SERP for the hashtag-count queries:

1. https://metricool.com/tiktok-hashtags/ — "3 to 5 hashtags per post usually works well", quality
   over quantity, "using too many hashtags can look spammy". Headings: How Hashtags Work on TikTok /
   Why Use Hashtags / How Many Should You Use / Tips & Best Practices / Hashtag Tools. No local or
   service-area angle. States "TikTok allows up to 100 characters in the caption" (outdated). Does
   not mention the 5-hashtag cap. Does not say TikTok publishes no recommended number.
2. https://www.juicer.io/blog/tiktok-hashtag-mistakes — "add 3 to 5 relevant tags to your caption
   before you post: one broad, two or three niche and, if it fits, one trending". Mentions
   geo-specific hashtags in one sentence with no examples. No maximum hashtag count. Ends in a
   product pitch for a social wall.
3. https://sproutsocial.com/insights/tiktok-hashtags/ — "3–5 per post", also floats a "3-3-3"
   formula allowing up to 9 hashtags, which the 5-cap now makes impossible. Useful line: "TikTok
   hashtags in the comments are less effective than in the caption. TikTok's search algorithm
   prioritizes the video description over the comments section." Cites Sprout Social Q2 2025 Pulse
   Survey. No local business angle.
4. https://www.capcut.com/resource/how-many-hashtags-should-i-use-on-tiktok — "Using 3 to 5 hashtags
   on TikTok usually gives the best results", because "adding too many can make your caption look
   messy and confuse the algorithm about your video's focus". Claims #fyp "signals TikTok to show
   your video to more people outside your followers", which contradicts TikTok's own description of
   the recommendation system. Advises against repeating the same hashtags every time. No local
   business angle.
5. https://hashtagtools.io/blog/tiktok-hashtags-fyp-viral-guide-2026 and
   https://blabla.ai/blog/how-many-hashtags-on-tiktok — same "3 to 5", virality framing, aimed at
   creators chasing the FYP, not at an owner with a service area.

What all of them miss:

- **The 5-hashtag cap.** TikTok has capped posts at 5 hashtags since August 2025. The top results
  still present "3 to 5" as a style preference the reader chooses, when the ceiling is now enforced
  in the app. The real question is no longer how many, it is which 5.
- **Nothing about a local service business.** None of them give a city tag, a trade tag or a
  service-area example. Advice is written for creators and ecommerce brands.
- **No honest weighting.** None of them say how small the measured hashtag effect is (~5% more
  views) relative to the caption and the video itself, so the reader over-invests in tags.
- **No reusable set.** None give an owner a fixed set to save once and a single slot to change per
  post, which is what someone posting 3 to 5 times a week actually needs.
- **#fyp.** Results either recommend it (CapCut) or hand-wave. None line it up against TikTok's own
  published description of what the recommendation system uses.

Facts deliberately left out:

- The TikTok caption character limit. Sources disagree (2,200 vs 4,000, with 2,200 reported for the
  posting API), so the draft states no number.
- "73% of TikTok users have discovered a local business through the app." Widely repeated with no
  traceable primary source.
- The SBE Council figures TikTok cites (84% say TikTok helped grow their business, 74% say it helps
  them connect with their local community). A survey commissioned by TikTok about TikTok; not used.
- Any claim that a city hashtag drives the local feed. The local feed is described as using location
  data, topic and recency, not hashtags, so the draft presents the city tag as a search and relevance
  play only.
- Hashtag post counts ("9 billion posts in #fyp", "a tag with 400 posts"). No source checked gives
  per-tag post counts, so the draft compares specific against broad without a number.
- The marginal value of the first hashtag against the fifth. The Metricool study compared posts with
  at least one hashtag against posts with none, so the draft states only that gap.
- Any claim about how much of a caption shows before "more", or that people search city and trade
  terms on TikTok. Neither has a traceable source here.
