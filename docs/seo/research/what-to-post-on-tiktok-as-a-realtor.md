## Top results (Google US, 2026-11-06)

1. theclose.com/real-estate-tiktok/ — "3 Top Agents on How to Get Likes & Leads": visual examples
   from named agents (Carys Eccleshall, Shawn King) but no attributed quotes in the text despite the
   title; lists home tours, tips, behind-the-scenes, neighborhood clips, before/after, market
   updates, Q&A, testimonials, dos and don'ts, trending challenges. No filming rules, no MLS/Clear
   Cooperation mention, no fair housing mention.
2. peachgum.ai, reel-e.ai, velisto.ai, momenzo.com, conbersa.ai — AI-tool blogs with idea lists (25
   to 35 items), one educational-answer format ("answer one question per video"), posting frequency
   "two or three videos a week for months," 30 seconds as optimal length. 37% US adult TikTok stat
   cited (Pew Research, matches our own sourced figure).
3. None of the above mention: what you cannot film in an occupied home before closing, that a public
   TikTok clip of an unlisted property counts as "public marketing" and can start the MLS Clear
   Cooperation filing clock, or fair housing wording rules applied to video captions/narration.

What they all miss: every one of the top results is an idea list with no mention of the two real
risks specific to realtors on video: (1) filming a home that is not yet in the MLS can trigger Clear
Cooperation's one-business-day filing requirement, same risk already documented for written posts in
our `coming-soon-real-estate-post` research; (2) Fair Housing wording rules apply to narration and
on-screen text, not just captions. No competitor connects TikTok content planning to either rule.

## Facts and sources (primary and reused)

| Claim in the article | Source |
| --- | --- |
| 37% of US adults use TikTok (already used, sourced, in `tiktok-for-plumbers.ts` and `real-estate-social-media-statistics.ts`) | Pew Research Center, 2025 survey of 5,022 US adults (Feb–June 2025): https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/ |
| Clear Cooperation Policy requires brokers to submit a listing to the MLS within one business day of starting public marketing; public marketing includes digital marketing on public-facing websites | NAR MLS Clear Cooperation Policy page: https://www.nar.realtor/about-nar/policies/mls-clear-cooperation-policy (reused from `coming-soon-real-estate-post` research, originally verified 2026-10-20) |
| One business day excludes Saturdays, Sundays and federal/state holidays | NAR FAQ March 2026, item 3c (same source set as above) |
| Clear Cooperation does not ban coming-soon marketing, only requires the MLS filing once public marketing starts | NAR statement 2026-03-20: https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/law-and-ethics/nar-releases-statement-on-pre-marketing-and-coming-soon-listings |
| The Fair Housing Act and 24 CFR § 100.75 make it illegal to publish any housing ad, in any medium, that indicates a preference or limitation based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status or national origin | 24 CFR § 100.75, Cornell LII: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.75 (reused from `real-estate-instagram-captions` research, verified 2026-11-02) |
| The regulation's test is describing the dwelling, not the resident: words like "perfect for families" or "safe neighborhood" are flagged; "two bedroom" or "quiet street" are not | Same regulation, HUD 1995 Achtenberg memo breakdown (reused source set) |

Inference, stated as advice not fact: since Clear Cooperation names "digital marketing on a public
facing website" as public marketing, treating a public TikTok clip of an unlisted home the same way
is a reasonable precaution, not a proven legal certainty; the article says so explicitly rather than
stating it as settled law.
