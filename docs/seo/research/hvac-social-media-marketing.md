5. housecallpro.com/resources/hvac-social-media-marketing/

What they all do: give a bullet list of 8-15 content *categories* (before/after, behind-the-scenes,
testimonials, memes, seasonal checklists, educational tips, promotions, community). Fetched in full:
hookagency.com/blog/hvac-social-media-post-ideas/ confirms 8 categories, **no example captions, no
posting-frequency number, and no safety/legal caution about refrigerant or DIY repair advice**.

Several sources (digitalauthority.me, among search snippets) explicitly suggest posting a "how to tell
if refrigerant is low" DIY tip video. This is the gap: EPA Section 608 requires certification to
service, maintain, repair or dispose of equipment that could release refrigerant (see Truth section
below), so recommending that a homeowner check their own refrigerant is advice an HVAC company
shouldn't be the one handing out. That is the differentiated angle: which "helpful tips" posts are
safe to give homeowners, and which cross into work that needs a certified tech, plus copy-paste
caption examples and a seasonal content calendar the top 5 don't provide.

## Facts and sources (every claim in the draft must trace to one of these)

1. **EPA Section 608 certification.** epa.gov/section608 (fetched 2026-10-31): Section 608 of the
   Clean Air Act makes it illegal to intentionally vent ozone-depleting refrigerants while
   maintaining, servicing, repairing or disposing of air conditioning or refrigeration equipment.
   epa.gov/section608/section-608-technician-certification-1 (fetched 2026-10-31), exact quote:
   "Technicians who maintain, service, repair, or dispose of equipment that could release ozone
   depleting refrigerants into the atmosphere must be certified." Checking refrigerant level requires
   opening a sealed system, which is servicing equipment that could release refrigerant.
   Source URLs: https://www.epa.gov/section608 and
   https://www.epa.gov/section608/section-608-technician-certification-1
2. **Air filter check/change interval.** energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling (fetched
   2026-10-31), exact quote: "Check your filter every month, especially during heavy use months
   (winter and summer). If the filter looks dirty after a month, change it. At a minimum, change the
   filter every 3 months." Source: https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling
3. **Smart thermostat savings.** Same energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling page: an ENERGY STAR
   certified smart thermostat saves "approximately $100 a year." Source:
   https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling
4. **TikTok Business Account music.** ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-use-the-commercial-music-library
   (reused citation, already in `social-media-for-restaurants.ts`): a Business Account only sees the
   Commercial Music Library when adding sound. Source:
   https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/how-to-use-the-commercial-music-library
5. **TikTok US reach.** Pew Research Center, "Americans' Social Media Use 2025" (reused citation,
   already in `tiktok-for-plumbers.ts`): 37% of US adults use TikTok. Source:
   https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/11/20/americans-social-media-use-2025/
6. **Seasonal search pattern.** Keyword Planner monthly search volumes for "hvac social media
   marketing" itself: 170 in March, 110 in April, versus 10-70 the rest of the year, i.e. spring is
   when HVAC owners look into this, matching the AC tune-up season. This is Next5's own research data
   (Keyword Planner pull above), not a web source.
