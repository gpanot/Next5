| # | Page | What it covers |

| 2 | showable.co, "25 Open House Social Media Post Ideas" | The closest competitor. 25 ideas, 5 caption formulas, 5 fill-in-the-blank templates, platform notes for Facebook / Instagram / LinkedIn / X, and a 2-weeks-out to post-event schedule. Fair housing wording is not mentioned. |
| 3 | saleswise.ai, "8 Unbeatable Open House Facebook Posts" | 8 post angles (virtual tour, CMA value, lifestyle, urgency, AI staging, testimonial, poll, multi-property). Mentions Facebook Events, a 3 to 5 day timeline and RSVP links. Fair housing is not mentioned. |
| 4 | blog.showami.com, "How to Promote an Open House on Social Media: a Checklist" | A five-phase checklist: one week before, 48 hours, one day before, day of, day after. No fair housing, no Facebook Events, no TikTok, no lead capture. |
| 5 | theclose.com, "26 Open House Ideas That Will Actually Get You Leads in 2026" | Only two social items: livestream the tour, and post hyperlocal (Facebook groups, Nextdoor). No captions, no schedule, no fair housing. |

Also read: coffeecontracts.com (Instagram-only, "comment OPEN" keyword play),
homelight.com and myrealpage.com (channel checklists).

What all five miss:

1. **Fair housing wording.** None of the five mentions that an open house post is an advertisement
   for the sale of a dwelling, so 42 U.S.C. 3604(c) and 24 CFR 100.75 apply to the caption and the
   photos. This is the one mistake in an open house caption that can cost an agent real money, and
   nobody in the top 5 says a word about it.
2. **Boosting advice that contradicts Meta's policy.** Several competitor pages (and the "boost a
   post targeting your ZIP code for $15 to $25" advice repeated across vendor blogs) do not mention
   that Meta requires US housing advertisers to self-identify as a Special Ad Category and run with
   approved targeting options. An agent who follows that advice as written is surprised when the
   targeting they wanted is not available.
3. **Exact, paste-ready caption text per post in a sequence.** showable.co gives formulas and
   fill-in-the-blanks; the rest give ideas with no copy at all. None gives one finished caption per
   post across a dated run-up.
4. **What to post when the open house was quiet.** Every page assumes a busy open house and a
   celebratory recap. None tells the agent what to post when three people came.

## 4. Every fact the article states, with its source

| Claim in the draft | Source (primary) |
| --- | --- |
| It is unlawful "to make, print, or publish, or cause to be made, printed, or published any notice, statement, or advertisement, with respect to the sale or rental of a dwelling that indicates any preference, limitation, or discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin, or an intention to make any such preference, limitation, or discrimination." | 42 U.S.C. 3604(c), quoted verbatim from https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3604 |
| The protected bases named in the statute are race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status and national origin. | 42 U.S.C. 3604(c), same source |
| HUD's rule covers "all written or oral notices or statements by a person engaged in the sale or rental of a dwelling", and written statements "include any applications, flyers, brochures, deeds, signs, banners, posters, billboards or any documents used with respect to the sale or rental of a dwelling." | 24 CFR 100.75(b), quoted verbatim from https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.75 (also https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/subtitle-B/chapter-I/part-100/subpart-B/section-100.75) |
| Discriminatory advertising includes "using words, phrases, photographs, illustrations, symbols or forms which convey that dwellings are available or not available to a particular group of persons because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin." | 24 CFR 100.75(c)(1), quoted verbatim, same source |
| Because the rule names words, phrases *and photographs*, the images in a post count, not only the caption. | 24 CFR 100.75(c)(1), same source. Direct reading of the quoted text. |
| Advertisers in the US, or targeting the US, Canada or certain European regions, who run housing ads must "self identify as a Special Ad Category" and use approved targeting options. | Meta Advertising Standards, Discriminatory Practices: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/unacceptable-content/discriminatory-practices/ |
| Meta's ad policy: "Ads must not discriminate or encourage discrimination against people based on personal attributes such as race, ethnicity, color, national origin, religion, age, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, family status, disability, medical or genetic condition." | Same Meta page, quoted verbatim |
| Facebook has an Events feature, reachable from Events in the left menu of your Feed. | https://www.facebook.com/help/210413455658361 (the help page's step 1 is "On your Feed, click Events in the left menu") |
