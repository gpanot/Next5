Also read: ripl.com, async.com, viraly.io, socialchamp.com, goodcirclemarketing.com, all at the same
idea-list altitude.

What all five miss:

1. **Real dates.** Not one of them names a single 2026 date. "Run a countdown" and "post your
   last-chance offer" are useless until you know that Small Business Saturday is November 28, that
   USPS recommends Ground Advantage by December 17, and that Christmas Day falls on a Friday this
   year, which changes which weekend is the last shopping weekend.
2. **Shipping and order cutoffs with actual numbers.** The one December post that reliably moves
   money for a small shop is "order by this date". Every top result says to post urgency; none of
   them tells the reader what date to put in it.
3. **The local service business.** The Next5 reader is often a plumber, a salon, a dentist or an
   auto repair shop with nothing to gift wrap. Four of the five top results are written for a brand
   with a product catalogue. A service business following them ends up posting a stock snow graphic.
4. **Holiday hours.** None of the five mentions the single highest-value December post for a local
   business: what your hours are on December 24, 25 and January 1, and setting the same thing as
   special hours on your Google Business Profile so the post and the map agree.
5. **Captions to copy.** All five give ideas and other brands' examples. None gives finished caption
   text an owner can paste and edit in 30 seconds.

of the year", two "it fills the week" / "gets more than a gift guide" outcome claims, a hashtag

Round 2 passed all 9 checks and reverified every weekday and date pair independently. It raised
five non-blocking notes; four were applied (the `answer` now says "expected delivery", the "that is
the day the money stops" clause is gone, the posts h2 no longer echoes the open house article's

shipped articles.

Round 2 also flagged a process fault worth keeping: the first version of this section was written
before round 2 ran and already said "Round 2 scored 9 of 9". A pre-recorded verdict reads as fact
to the next run's reviewer. Write this section after the review, not before.
