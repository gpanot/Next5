## Facts and sources
1. **HIPAA authorization is required before a covered healthcare provider (including a dental practice) posts an identifiable patient's photo, name, or testimonial for marketing/social media use.** The authorization must be in writing, specifically describe the marketing/social media use, include an expiration, a signature, and a statement of the right to revoke. A covered entity cannot bundle this authorization into a general treatment consent form, and cannot condition treatment on the patient signing it.
   - Primary source: 45 CFR 164.508 (HIPAA Privacy Rule, authorization requirements), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.508
   - Cross-checked secondary summary (accurate subsection citations, 164.508(b)(3) bundling restriction, 164.508(b)(4) conditioning restriction): https://www.accountablehq.com/post/45-cfr-164-508-explained-when-hipaa-requires-patient-authorization
   - Note: attempted to fetch hhs.gov and the ecfr.gov page directly; both blocked the fetch tool (403, and a suspicious redirect to an unrelated "unblock.federalregister.gov" domain which was not followed). Citing the regulation itself (45 CFR 164.508) as the source link; the specific requirements above (written, describes use, expiration, signature, revocation right, no bundling, no conditioning treatment) are confirmed by the secondary legal-compliance source which quotes the CFR subsections directly.
2. Posting cadence commonly recommended across dental marketing sites: 3-5 times per week, consistency over frequency. (insidea.com, dentalbase.ai) — general industry advice, not a regulated fact, used as plain advice not as a hard claim.
3. Content types that recur across all sources as working for dental practices: oral hygiene tips, myth vs fact, team/behind-the-scenes, before-after (with consent), patient testimonials/reviews, office tour, polls/Q&A stickers, holiday posts.
   - Sources: https://insidea.com/blog/marketing/dentists/social-media-post-idea/ , https://www.getweave.com/dentist-social-media-posts/ , https://somanymolars.com/social-media-posts-dental-offices/
4. What you can post without patient-consent issues: team photos, office environment/equipment, educational content about procedures without patient identifiers, community involvement, dentist credentials/accomplishments. What requires consent: any identifiable patient (face, voice, name) in a photo, video, testimonial, or before/after pair.
   - Source: https://www.aidelly.ai/guides/social-media-marketing-for-dentists (cross-checked against the HIPAA authorization requirement above)
