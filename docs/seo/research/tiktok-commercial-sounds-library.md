Read in full: result 1 and result 6.

Result 1 (Soundstripe) covers why Business Accounts see only the CML, licensing tiers, how to switch
back to Personal, third-party licensed music, original audio, and enforcement. Checked directly: it
gives **no advice specific to a small local business**, **no guidance on finding a usable track
inside the CML**, and **no mention of photo slideshows**.

Result 6 (Third Chair) is written for brand and agency rights teams: clearance logs, sound IDs,
Spark Ads, influencer campaigns. Checked directly: **nothing for a local owner**, **no guidance on
picking a track**, no coverage of original sound.

What they all miss, and what this article does:

- The restriction follows the **use**, not the account badge. TikTok's US Music Terms of Service say
  music from Sounds is for personal, non-commercial purposes and should not be used to promote a
  brand or business without the necessary rights. So "switch to a Creator account to get the
  trending sounds" is not a fix for a promotional post. The top results treat account type as the
  whole story; one mentions intent in passing, none makes it the answer.
- How to actually find a track inside the CML (All music / Top Music ranked by popularity,
  Playlists, filters, and the desktop Creative Center browser) instead of scrolling the phone.
- What to do with the trend when the audio is locked: your own recorded sound and voiceover, which
  the slideshow and video editors both offer.
- Photo slideshows, which is how most Next5 readers post.
